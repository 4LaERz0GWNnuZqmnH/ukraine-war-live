Methodology

Ukraine War Live is a fully automated, serverless OSINT aggregation platform. There is no human in the loop: no analyst writes, edits, curates, or approves any event on this site. It runs on Cloudflare's edge network and refreshes every 2 hours (00:00–22:00 UTC).

It is not real-time, predictive, or authoritative. Every event is a machine reading of a public news headline and should be treated as a lead, not a fact. Any casualty figures are numbers announced by one side in the war — Ukraine or Russia — passed through without independent checking. They are not confirmed death or injury tolls.

1. Data pipeline

  1. RSS ingestion. A fixed set of ~13 feeds (major international wires and Ukrainian outlets in English) is polled concurrently each run. Items older than 96 hours (4 days) are discarded; at most 90 unseen items proceed per run.
  2. AI extraction. Headlines and short summaries are passed to a large language model (Llama 3.3 70B on Cloudflare Workers AI) at temperature 0.1 with a strict schema. The model is instructed never to invent a source URL and never to guess coordinates. Output is capped at 20 strike events and 12 ground events per run.
  3. Validation & geocoding. Malformed objects are dropped. Coordinates outside a Ukraine + western-Russia bounding box are nulled. For events the model left without coordinates, a static gazetteer of ~110 places (oblast centres, front-line towns, Russian target cities) fills in a settlement-centroid position.
  4. Deduplication. Two signatures per event — an exact source-URL hash, and event type + 0.5° grid cell + time bucket (1 hour for strikes, 4 hours for territorial change). If either is already seen, the event is dropped. Signatures are retained for 30 days.
  5. Archive. Surviving events are appended as NDJSON — one JSON object per line — to a per-day key in Cloudflare KV, with a running date→count index. This is the uncapped canonical record, downloadable at /archive/<date>.
  6. Distribution. A merged JSON snapshot of the most recent 200 events per pipeline is cached to Cloudflare KV and served at /feed.json. No alerting, no social channels.

3. Ground-operations pipeline

A separate worker, on its own schedule, with its own prompt, extracts only ground_engagement and territorial_change events — infantry, armour and artillery combat at named places, and settlements captured, lost, or claimed. It explicitly ignores airstrikes, missiles, drones, air defence, naval action, and diplomacy, which the main pipeline handles.

4. Front-line layer

The red occupied-territory shading on the map is a third worker that pulls the current control map from DeepStateMap.live every 6 hours, keeps only the occupied / Crimea / ORDLO polygons, and caches the trimmed GeoJSON. It can be several hours stale and is © DeepStateMap.live, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. It is independent of the event pipeline.

5. Data architecture

  • Cloudflare KV (one namespace) — deduplication signatures and processed-URL set (30-day window), the 200-event live feed per pipeline, the per-day NDJSON archive, the front-line GeoJSON, and run diagnostics.
  • Caps — 20 strikes/run, 12 ground/run, 200 events per pipeline in the live feed; the archive is uncapped.
  • Machine-readable — /feed.json, /api/events?type=&tier=&since=, /archive/index.json, /archive/<date>, /frontline.json, /status, /llms.txt.

6. Editorial policy

The platform is politically agnostic by construction. The extractor records attacker, target, and location regardless of the source's alignment, and applies the same confidence rules to every side. There is no affiliation with any government, military, or advocacy organisation. There are no ads and no third-party trackers. Only public officials and official spokespeople are named; no private individuals.

7. Known limitations

  • Headline-only extraction can misplace, conflate, or misattribute events. The temperature, hard caps, schema validation, and "never invent" instructions reduce this; they do not eliminate it.
  • Coordinates are settlement-level at best and are omitted when uncertain.
  • Deduplication is lossy in both directions — it can miss near-duplicates and can occasionally merge two distinct events.
  • Casualty figures are numbers announced by one side (Ukraine or Russia), passed through unverified. They are shown with whoever reported them and are never added together into an overall total.
  • Coverage is only as good as the feed list and the news cycle; quiet or poorly reported events are simply absent.
